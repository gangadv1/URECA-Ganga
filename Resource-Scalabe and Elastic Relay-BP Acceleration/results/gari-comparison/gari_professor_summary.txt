GARI and BA2 improve different layers of the decoder. GARI changes the correlated detector graph itself: it rewrites the X/Y/Z problem into D_X, D_Z, U, and V blocks so normalized min-sum BP sees fewer harmful Y-related four-cycles, then gives those blocks specialized serial and parallel FPGA engines. BA2 leaves the Relay-BP graph and equations unchanged and only rearranges fault/message addresses so four folded lanes collide less often in modulo-four banks. GARI therefore targets inference structure and whole-core hardware reuse, while BA2 targets storage layout inside a fixed folded access model. GARI reports about 596 ns per syndrome round for a 24-decoder, eight-FPGA ensemble at p=0.001; BA2 currently reports only relative software-modeled cycle reductions, so neither speed nor resource superiority can be claimed. The defensible contribution is a bank-aware folded memory/layout optimization for Relay-BP that is complementary to GARI's graph transformation.
